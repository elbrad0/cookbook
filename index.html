Brad's Cookbook

“I hate how vague recipes can be about temperature. Medium means nothing to me.”

So every heat step in these recipes tells you what to look, listen and smell for instead: the sound of the pan, how the bubbles behave, what colour things should turn. Each step also says why it works, so when something goes differently in your kitchen you can tell what to change.

Recipes

  • Chilli con Carne

    Mince browned hard, onions cooked in its fat, and a long, slow simmer. Serves ~4 · about 2 hours, mostly simmering

  • Mexican Rice with Black Beans

    Rice toasted with fried spices, simmered in stock, with black beans steamed on top. Serves 6 as a side · about 45 min

  • Carbonara

    Smoked streaky bacon, egg yolks, Pecorino and Parmigiano, with the pan cooled before the egg goes in. Serves 1 · about 25 min

How to use these pages

Cue rows

Every heat step has coloured rows telling you what you should see or hear, and what to do if it's off:

RightWhat it should look, sound or smell like. Carry on.
Too hotThe signs it's running hot, and what to do about it.
Too coolThe signs it needs more heat.
Burner chips

Smallest burner · lowest

The blue chip on a step says which burner to use and roughly where to set it. It's a starting point: the cue rows have the final say.

Ticking off steps

Tick each step as you finish it, so you can see where you are at a glance. Ticks are saved only in your own browser, on that device, so they're still there if you close the page mid-cook. "Clear all ticks" resets them.

Measurements

Metric throughout. Spices are given by weight and by spoon; spoons are level standard measuring spoons (5 ml teaspoon, 15 ml tablespoon), not cutlery.
