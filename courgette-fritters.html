Courgette Fritters

Crisp outside, soft inside, with Parmigiano and a little chilli heat. The whole recipe comes down to getting water out of the courgette, then pressing the fritters thin so the middle cooks through by the time the outside is golden.

Makes about 10 ~25 min prep ~20 min frying, in batches
The one thing that matters most. Courgette is mostly water. Any water left in the mix turns to steam in the pan, which keeps the fritters soft and pale and stops them crisping. Salt draws the water out, squeezing removes it, and the flour and breadcrumbs soak up the rest. Squeeze harder than feels necessary.

Ingredients

500 gCourgettes, firmAbout 2–3 medium. Soft, older courgettes hold more water and squeeze out mushy.
½ tsp · 3 gSalt, for drawing out water
1½ tbsp · 12 gPlain flour
1½ tbsp · 8 gDried breadcrumbsShop-bought, or homemade (see Notes).
3 tbsp · 15 gParmigiano Reggiano, finely grated
¼ tsp · 0.5 gChilli flakesThis is mild. Add more if you like heat.
¼ tsp heaped · 1 gGarlic powder or granules
small pinchSalt, for the mixtureKeep it small: some of the first salt stays in the courgette.
1Medium egg
~1 tbsp a batchOlive oil, for fryingExtra virgin is fine at this heat. Refined avocado oil works too.

Spoon amounts are the main measure. Weights are approximate, because flour, crumbs and grated cheese pack differently.

Kit and heat

Box grater, coarse side
Colander over a bowl or the sink
Nut milk bag or muslin for squeezing
Large mixing bowl
Non-stick frying pan and a fish slice
Plate lined with kitchen paper
Why not a tea towel. It works, but washed towels can carry detergent and fabric-softener perfume into the courgette, which you press hard against wet fabric. A nylon nut milk bag rinses clean and holds nothing. Boil a new one in plain water for a few minutes before its first use.
On gas: use a small or medium burner and keep the flame under the base of the pan. If your pan is wider than the flame, the middle is the hot spot, so keep the fritters there or swap them round.
Electric, ceramic or induction: the cues are the same. Go by the oil in step 5, not the setting.

Method

  1. no heat

    Put the flour, breadcrumbs, Parmigiano, chilli flakes, garlic and the small pinch of salt in the large bowl. Stir them together. Leave the egg until step 4.

    Doing this first means nothing needs measuring once the courgette is ready.

  2. no heat10 min resting

    Trim the ends; there's no need to peel. Grate on the coarse side straight into the colander. Sprinkle over the ½ tsp of salt, toss with your hands, and leave for 10 minutes.

    RightWater beading on the shreds and dripping out of the colander.
    why salt first

    Salt on the outside of the courgette's cells pulls water out of them by osmosis. The squeeze in the next step then gets far more out than it would from unsalted courgette.

  3. no heat~5 min

    Put two or three handfuls in the bag at a time, not the whole lot. Over the sink, gather the top closed and twist it round and round so it tightens on the courgette. Tip each squeezed batch into the bowl of dry ingredients.

    RightOnly the odd drip comes out however hard you twist, and the courgette feels damp rather than wet.
    Not yetA steady trickle still comes out. Keep twisting.
    why batches

    With the whole lot in the bag, twisting compresses the outside and leaves a wet core in the middle. A smaller amount gets squeezed evenly all the way through.

  4. no heat

    Crack in the egg and mix well with your hands, so every strand is coated.

    RightA heaped spoonful pressed together holds its shape.
    Too wetIt slumps or looks wet. Mix in another ½ tbsp of flour.
    what each part does

    The egg sets in the heat and glues the strands together. The flour and breadcrumbs absorb any water the squeeze missed. The Parmigiano adds salt and browns well.

  5. small–medium burner · low-isha few minutes

    Put about 1 tbsp of oil in the cold pan, then light the burner, and tilt the pan to coat the base. Let the oil come up gently so you see each stage arrive.

    ColdThick; sits in a pool when you tilt the pan.
    ReadyThinner, runs freely, and the surface shimmers with faint wavy ripples, like heat haze over a road. A pinch of mixture dropped in gives a steady, lively sizzle with small bubbles round it.
    Too hotThin wisps of smoke, and a sharp, acrid smell in place of a grassy, peppery one. A test pinch crackles, spits and browns in seconds. Lift the pan off for 30 seconds and turn the flame down.

    If there's no shimmer and no sizzle after about 5 minutes, turn the heat up a notch.

    why stop at the shimmer

    Extra virgin olive oil starts to smoke at about 190–210 °C. Fritters fry well at about 160–180 °C, which is the shimmer stage, so there's a safe margin as long as you stop there. Smoke means the oil is breaking down and will taste bitter. Putting the oil in before the heat means you watch it climb instead of overshooting in an empty pan.

  6. same burner · keep the sizzle steady~2–4 min a side

    Scoop about 2 heaped tablespoons per fritter. Put 2 or 3 in the pan with space between them, and press each one thin, about 5 mm, with the back of the spoon or the fish slice. Cook until the underside is golden and crisp, then flip once and do the other side.

    RightA constant, steady sizzle, the underside golden and crisp, and the shreds round the edge going lacy and brown.
    Too hotDark brown in under a minute while the middle is still raw, or smoke. Lift the pan off and turn the flame down.
    Too coolThe sizzle fades, or still pale after 4 minutes. They'll soak up oil and go greasy. Turn the heat up a notch.

    Check the first batch: break one open. The egg in the middle should look opaque and set, with no glossy, wet patches. If it's still soft, press the next batch thinner or give them a little longer.

    why thin

    Browning happens at the surface, but the egg has to set in the middle, and heat reaches the middle by conduction. The time that takes rises with the square of the thickness, so a fritter half as thick sets in about a quarter of the time. At 1 cm the outside can turn golden while the centre is still soft and eggy. At 5 mm, golden on the outside and set in the middle arrive together.

  7. same burner

    Move the fritters to the lined plate. Wipe out stray bits of courgette or cheese with a folded sheet of kitchen paper (the pan is hot), add a fresh tablespoon of oil, wait for the shimmer again, and fry the next batch.

    why wipe

    Leftover shreds and cheese burn and smoke at normal frying heat even when the oil itself is fine, and they make the next batch taste bitter.

  8. no heat

    Eat them hot with a dip of red pesto loosened with olive oil and lemon juice. I don't measure it: start with a heaped tablespoon of pesto, a tablespoon of oil and a good squeeze of lemon, and adjust until it's loose enough to dip into.

Rescue

Falling apart in the pan

The mix is too wet. Squeeze the rest of the batch again, or mix in another ½ tbsp of flour before the next batch.

Golden outside, soft and eggy inside

Too thick. Press the next ones thinner rather than turning the heat up, which only browns the outside faster.

Greasy and pale

The oil wasn't hot enough when they went in. Wait for the shimmer and the test-pinch sizzle before each batch.

Notes

  • Homemade breadcrumbs: grate stale bread straight from the freezer on the box grater; frozen bread shreds cleanly. Toast the crumbs in a dry pan on low, stirring, for about 5 minutes, until dry and sandy and just starting to colour. Fresh crumbs still hold water, and here their job is to soak it up.
  • Filling: they're more filling than they look. A full batch made a big lunch for one.
  • Freezing: cool completely and freeze flat in a bag. Reheat from frozen in a single layer in a hot oven until crisp again, about 20–25 minutes.
