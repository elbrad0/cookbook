Serves ~4 · about 2 hours, mostly simmering · better the next day

Chilli con carne

Brown the mince hard, cook the onions in its fat, and give it a long simmer. Every heat step comes with what to look and listen for, because "medium" means something different on every hob.

Kit and heat

  • A heavy-based pot of about 3 litres, with a lid. A thick base spreads heat evenly, so the sauce doesn't catch. Stainless steel is best: the meat sticks a little and leaves brown residue on the base, and that residue becomes flavour. Non-stick browns less well and shouldn't go on high heat empty.
  • A bowl for the browned mince, a jug for the sauce mix, and a scale. A small 0.1 g digital scale is ideal for the spices, but measuring spoons work too; both are given below.
  • Hobs vary a lot. On some, the lowest setting of the biggest burner is still very hot. Go by the cues in each step, not by the knob position.
  • Burners: brown and fry on a medium or large burner. Simmer on your smallest burner, on its lowest setting.
  • Electric, ceramic or induction: the cues are the same. Ceramic and electric plates are slow to cool when you turn them down, so lift the pot off the heat for a moment if something is catching.

Mince: 20% fat is what makes this work. The fat it gives off cooks the onions, so there's no oil, and fattier mince stays tender through a long simmer. Lean mince dries out with long cooking; if that's all you have, add 1 tbsp of oil for the onions.

Chillies: 2 large mild red chillies give a gentle heat. For more, add 1–2 small finger or bird's-eye chillies, which are much hotter. Most of a chilli's heat is in the white membrane inside, so leave it in for more heat or scrape it out for less.

Prep

Cooking

  1. Medium or large burner · high
    ReadyFlick in a few drops of water. They bead into balls and skate about. A thin layer of steam lifts them, which only happens at roughly 190 °C or more.
    Not yetThe drops hiss and spread flat. Wait and test again.
    Very hotThe drops skate very fast and vanish almost at once. Turn the heat down a little before the mince goes in.
  2. Same burner · high, or as the preheat set it
    RightA loud, steady crackle; a dark brown crust underneath when you lift an edge (about 3–4 min). Then break it up and give it about 2 min more.
    SteamingQuiet bubbling, liquid pooling. The pot is too full or not hot enough. Stop stirring and let the liquid boil off; the crackle comes back once it has.
    Too hotSmoke, or black bits.

    Smoke or steam? You get a big burst of steam the moment the mince goes in. Steam: white, gone within a hand's width of the pot, no smell beyond cooking meat. Fine. Smoke: bluish-grey, drifts around the room, sharp acrid smell, can sting your eyes. Turn it down.

    Why two batches: too much meat at once releases water faster than it can boil off, so the mince steams grey instead of browning. Tip each batch into the bowl and do the water-drop test again before the second. Keep any juice that collects in the bowl, as it all goes back in.

  3. Same burner · well down; set by the cue
    RightA soft fizz, small bubbles around the pieces; soft and translucent in 10–15 min.
    Too hotThe edges brown in the first few minutes.
    Too coolNo sound at all.
  5. Same burner, same heat
    RightThe smell of the spices blooms.
    Too hotThe spices darken fast, or there's a sharp, acrid smell. Turn the heat down at once.
  6. RightIt goes from bright red to a darker rust colour.
    Too hotBlack patches.
  8. Smallest burner · lowest

    Check again after 2 minutes. A lid traps heat, so a gentle simmer can build into a boil.

  9. RightLift the lid to check: anything from a few lazy bubbles to steady bubbling is fine, as long as nothing catches on the base.
    Too hotThe whole surface churning, the lid rattling, or the scorch check snags. Set the lid slightly ajar to let some heat escape as steam.
    Too coolNo bubbles at all.

    Scorch check each time you stir: drag the spoon across the base. If it snags, or brown flakes come up smelling burnt, turn it down and don't scrape those bits in.

    It will look runny at this stage. A lidded pot loses little water; the next step thickens it.

  10. Smallest burner · same, or up a little
    DoneDrag the spoon across the base: the gap stays open for 1–2 seconds.
    Not yetThe gap closes at once. Keep simmering and test again.
    Too hotIt spatters out of the pot.
